## 3. Problem and product
The problem: Shopify merchants need to increase onsite conversion and average order value without adding headcount or custom development. Static merchandising and one-size-fits-all cart and product pages leave revenue on the table when recommendations and offers are not personalized at the point of decision.  

The product: Rebuy provides AI-driven product recommendations, upsell and cross-sell offers, merchandising and search, and a “Smart Cart” that can present dynamic incentives and bundles inside the cart experience—aimed at lifting conversion and AOV for Shopify stores (https://rebuyengine.com). Positioning and features are native to the Shopify ecosystem, reducing integration overhead versus stitching together point tools.

## 12. Risks and how they ease
- Platform dependency: Concentration on Shopify platform rules and APIs. Eases with multi-product depth that is resilient to policy shifts, outstanding merchant retention, and proven ROI that justifies price.  
- Competitive intensity: Personalization, search, and upsell are crowded categories. Eases with clear win rates in head-to-heads, differentiated product surface area (e.g., Smart Cart execution), and integration breadth within Shopify.  
- ROI attribution: Merchants scrutinize paid apps; attribution can be challenged by overlapping tools. Eases with transparent measurement, controlled tests, and case studies verified by reference customers.

The question the founder would least like asked: What is net revenue retention and logo retention by cohort for the last four quarters, and how do they trend?

## 13. Next steps
- Product and customer diligence: 3–5 reference calls across merchant sizes and verticals; review activation time, realized ROI, and stack overlaps.  
- Numbers: Cohort analyses (logo and net revenue retention), gross margin by module, CAC payback by channel, and month-by-month burn and runway.  
- Team and references: Founder and key exec references; assessment of Shopify ecosystem relationships and roadmap credibility.

## Questions for Founders
- Recommendation
1. What are the one or two metrics you hold the team accountable to each quarter, and why?

- Market and timing
2. What market size do you prioritize for planning: Shopify merchants in your target tiers, or a broader e‑commerce TAM? What sourced figures underpin it?
3. What changed in the last two years (Shopify APIs, ad costs, merchant behavior) that materially improved your win rates or expansion?

- Traction
4. What are current ARR, number of paying customers, and logo retention at 12 months?
5. What is net revenue retention by cohort for the last four quarters?

- Business model and path to profit
6. What is your pricing model (tiers, usage, add‑ons) and average ACV by segment?
7. What are gross margins by product module?
8. What is blended CAC, payback period, and primary acquisition channel?
9. What cash is on the balance sheet, current monthly burn, and months of runway? Can you reach profitability without another large round, and by when?

- The founders
10. Who are the founders and key executives, and what prior outcomes or Shopify ecosystem roles are most relevant?

- Competition
11. Who do you most often see in competitive deals, and what is your head‑to‑head win rate?
12. What features or integrations make customers stick, and what are the main reasons for churn?

- The round
13. Are you raising now? If so, what is the round size, structure, and target valuation?
14. What would be an ideal role and check size for Sidekick in this round?

- Why Sidekick
15. Which 3–5 merchants or partners from the Sidekick network would be most impactful introductions in the next 60 days?
